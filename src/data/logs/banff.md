### Day 2: Lake Louise

Lake Louise is _really_ **Blue**. The type of blue you'd think all bodies of water look like as a kid. The type of blue you'd want to drink to quench your 3AM thirst. Honestly, there should be a color to describe the specific blue.

![Lake Louise](/images/logs/banff/DSCF0308.webp)

#### **Why is Lake Louise blue?**

Short tangent but I found it interesting to learn that the blue color these lakes get are from a phenomenon that occurs when there's a glacier that _feeds_ into the lake. Essentially, the glacier grinds against the mountain it's on which creates this **_Rock Flour_**. Once these extremely fine particles of rock enter the lake, they stay suspended instead of sinking due to their tiny size. Once these particles are hit with sunlight, they scatter the blue and green wavelengths of light, creating **Lake Louise Blue**.

###

Originally we simply wanted to see Lake Louise, maybe cycle around, then head on to another area (possibly Banff city). We reach Louise and it starts to rain. We want to make the most of this stop so we start our walk around the lake. Once we reach the end, we see a board indicating that there's a hike that goes behind Lake Louise named [The Plain of Six Glaciers](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/canada/alberta/plain-of-six-glaciers-trail). Now I heard that cool ass name and knew we had to give it a shot. Despite the rain, almost single-digit temperature, and little to no gear, we gave it a shot.

After a grueling but stunning 3 hour hike through gravel and occasional snow to the peak, we finally made it. There was a cool shack at the top selling soup, tea, coffee, P&B sandwiches, etc. Not sure whose idea that was but they're geniuses - we were all starving by then.

### Day 3: Class IV+ White Water Rafting

![Before](/images/logs/banff/rafting-start.webp)

I always considered myself a good swimmer and not one to be scared of water but this honestly got me. Our instructor **Dean** (rightmost in the picture) was quite a character. He talked about how this is his first time rafting (joke of course). He'd been rafting for 22 years, that too all over the globe from India to South Africa. Throughout the session he'd yell "Yeehaw", never thought I'd hear that unironically and think it was cool...

Dean had 1 and only 1 rule for us: listen and apply all commands that come out of his mouth. The main commands were:

- Forward + Backward
- Left + Right
  - all 4 of these had a **"HARD!"** alternate which indicated extreme danger
- Hold on
  - this indicated stopping paddling and gripping the rope that covered the entire middle of the raft
- Get Down
  - this was the scary one. it meant we stop paddling, jump in the boat and hold on for dear life. issue was that he'd need us back on the paddle within 1-2 seconds, it's harder than it sounds in a slippery boat with a person right next to you

Not to flex or anything but Dean had me in the front of the raft even though he knew I hadn't ever rafted before. His mentality was that he needed someone "strong" in the front so he chose me. Little did he know...

###

Rafting comes in 4 (legal) classes of technical difficulty:

- Class I: Easy
  - fast moving water but no obstructions
  - basic paddling
  - low risk
- Class II: Novice
  - entering rapids but wide and clear; medium-sized waves
  - basic maneuvering
  - still low risk
- Class III: Intermediate
  - rapids with irregular waves capable of swamping an open canoe
  - good paddle control & ability to follow commands quick!
- Class IV: Advanced
  - intense rapids with large waves capable of capsizing a raft
  - precise boat control, strong physical fitness, and mandatory rescue skills

We knew that this river was a Class IV at its worst but Dean informed us that thanks to the rain the day before, we'd be in something called a **Class IV+**.

###

Honestly, the experience was something I'd recommend to everyone, no matter the age or fitness level. Rafting is a team sport which requires the whole raft to coordinate as one unit. Dean is only as good as his team and we are only good if we listen which we did.

Towards the end of the session, there came a reasonably steady part of the river. Dean told us that if we were thinking of getting in the river and getting a feel of it, it's now or never. My ego was inflated with my great swimming skills from the past so I took him up on that along with 2 other people. The moment I got in, I felt my feet sweep under the raft, almost as if I was about to get sucked under it. The water was unimaginably quick and fierce.

Within a minute, I decided enough was enough and that it was time to get out. On my first attempt, I was all the way up but the damn raft had nothing to grab on to on the inside so I fell back in. I tried 2-3 more times after which my legs gave out and I could not push anymore. Luckily, the guy sitting opposite to me was kind enough to lift me out of the water. That experience was pretty awakening.

### Day 7: Boo the bear

This day we just spent our time at the Kicking Horse Resort. There was a sky bridge nearby too with a huge suspension bridge and some other rope climbing type activities but the highlight of the day was visiting Boo the bear.

![Boo the bear](/images/logs/banff/IMG_9126.webp)
If you take a look at my pics for the day, you'll see that I was particularly fond of Boo. Boo has an [entire history](https://kickinghorseresort.com/purchase/boo-grizzly-bear/). The main reason Boo is still in captivity is that he's too friendly with humans. You may wonder why this is an issue. Now imagine a 300kg grizzly bear charging at you... will you think twice what its intentions are?

The crazy thing is that you wouldn't expect a grizzly bear to be so quick that you need to jog to keep up with its walking pace.

## Overview

So then why did I like this trip so much? It ties back to the consciousness thing I mentioned earlier: I got to be present for once.

### Family

My uncle had recently moved to the Middle East from North America. We used to spend a lot of time with him and his family so it was great seeing them there. There's just something poetic about 19 people coming together to give each other time and connect.

A huge part is that I think I've taken half of them for granted most of my life. For example, I had never sat down with my grandpa and asked him about his life. It's always him asking me how work/school/life is but never me understanding him. He gave me a run down of his childhood. My dad told stories about his own grandpa who migrated from India to Pakistan during Pakistan's independence, grandma told traumatic stories of her own, all new stories I had never heard of before.

### Nature

Though I've been trying to change this, I wouldn't consider myself a very "fit" or active person. I do however love the outdoors. Ever since we moved to Canada, the first time I smelt the fresh air coming from the trees I got addicted. There's no explaining the difference, if you ever go to Qatar/the Middle East, you'll just feel it.

The great outdoors got my ass. The hikes, camping, the wildlife, is just all so cool to see. Now take that and amplify it by a thousandfold. You get mountains with full forests. Full forests with full habitats and untouched ecosystems. Instead of taking over the area, we're just passing through for once.

![Bob](/images/logs/banff/bear.webp)
Deer roam around as if **they** are the predators rather than us. The first bear we saw was an adolescent it seemed who was much more scared of us than we were of it. A few days later though, we saw an adult with scars on its face, casually strolling on the side of the highway. The only way to spot a bear in the wild was to stop wherever you saw a pile of cars with their hazards on at a random part of the highway.

I specifically like bears. They are just so nonchalant and self-focused. They don't attack, they don't even look at you. They simply exist and care about themselves. All they do is eat and sleep. Since they're omnivores, they don't even have preferences.

Due to social media or whatever it is, I think so many of us are people pleasers. We feel the need to give up our own desires for a random stranger we'll never see again in our lives. Now don't read this and start cussing at strangers, that's definitely not what I'm saying. Just don't catch yourself valuing others' desires over your own, at the end of the day it's your life not theirs. **Be a bear**.

### Away from tech

My whole life revolves around computers and tech. Don't get me wrong, I absolutely love what I do but it does get overwhelming at times. I always joke about how I wanna run away to a remote part of the world and start a goat farm maybe with some horses and sheep. Geese would be cool too.

My screen time is embarrassingly high. I delete Instagram every few weeks to get off reels but always end up going back. There's many reasons for this too but more on that later.

The point here is that Banff was an escape. The cottage I stayed at even had a guitar, I'd just spend my afternoon sitting on the porch vibing as everyone got ready for the day. My mind always feels clearer without the constant rush of information/entertainment from my phone and devices. After a week without tech, it felt even better but then work started and I fell back into the loop. My next personal goal is to reduce my screen time and just be more present regularly for my own good. The brain rot on reels just resonates with me on a deeper level I guess.

I wish I could just run away and start a goat farm.
